\subsection{Mapping Private Infrastructure}

We found a large corpus of testing (MCCs 001 and 991), private (MCC 999), and
unassigned MCCs described in \S\ref{sec:background} in our dataset. Canonically,
testing MCCs support experimental networks, private MCCs identify infrastructure
intended for internal use, and unassigned MCCs define no purpose. However, our
results show that all three types can reveal the locations and characteristics
of potentially private infrastructure and activities.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{tesla_gigafactory_private_mcc_map.pdf}
    \caption{Testing and private MCC identities reveal sensitive commercial infrastructure and activities.}
    \label{fig:commercial-sensitive-mcc-maps}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:commercial-sensitive-mcc-maps} shows that the same exposure extends to commercial and
critical infrastructure. Private MCC identities delineate networks at the Cadia mine (11 LTE identities;
5,334 observations), Tesla Gigafactory Berlin (20 NR identities; 8,865 observations), the Applus+ IDIADA
automotive proving ground (11 GSM/UMTS identities; 4,622 observations), and Point Beach Nuclear Plant (four
NR identities; 760 observations). These records reveal not only the presence of internal cellular systems,
but also their technology generation, physical footprint, and persistence—information that may disclose
facility capabilities, testing activity, or operational changes. Thus, even when an MCC is correctly used
for a private network, making its observations globally discoverable creates a privacy and security risk
for the organization operating it.

Figure~\ref{fig:military-defense-mcc-maps} demonstrates that atypical MCCs can act as signatures of
otherwise non-public infrastructure. We observe an unassigned 009/09 NR identity at the DGA Landes missile
test range (578 observations), testing and unassigned identities at Idaho National Laboratory (five
identities; 1,033 observations), six testing 001/01 LTE identities at Porton Down (688 observations), and
five testing 001/01 LTE and NR identities at Stockbridge (1,215 observations). Their repeated observation
and spatial concentration within research and defense facilities reveal the location, radio technology, and
approximate extent of networks that may have been intended only for local or experimental use. Although
these associations do not establish network ownership or purpose, they show that globally collected
cellular metadata can expose sensitive infrastructure without requiring access to subscriber traffic.

    \caption{Testing, private, and unassigned MCC identities at four research, industrial, and defense sites.}
    \label{fig:military-defense-mcc-maps}
\end{figure}


\subsection{Out-of-Country \acp{MCC}}


Valid nationally assigned MCC can be still be a signature of adversarial
activity when it appears outside the country that administers it. Although some
mismatches arise from legacy allocations or identifier aliasing, spatially
concentrated mismatches can be a side effect of private or sensitive sites and
activities. We analyzed a variety of examples where out-of-country MCC placement
could be used to discover unusual operator activity.

\subsection{Contested Territories}

Cellular infrastructure can reveal adversarial activity beyond attacks on the
network itself, because any event that impacts network operation and
availability may leave observable infrastructure-level signatures. One example
of this phenomena is terratorial sovereignty: political control over a region
includes its communications infrastructure and carries the responsibility for
its administration. Thus, the geographic distribution of cellular activity can
implicate political control over those regions, which is a particularly useful
basis for investigating regions subject to competing claims of sovereignty.

We examine the geographic and temporal distribution of cellular activity in
contested regions of Eastern Europe and The Levant. In Ukraine, we observe that
Russian administered cellular infrastructure follows the front lines
geographically.
