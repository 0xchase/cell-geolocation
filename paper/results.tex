\subsection{Mapping Private Infrastructure}

We found a large corpus of testing (MCCs 001 and 991), private (MCC 999), and
unassigned MCCs described in \S\ref{sec:background} in our dataset. Canonically,
testing MCCs support experimental networks, private MCCs identify infrastructure
intended for internal use, and unassigned MCCs define no purpose. However, our
results show that all three types can reveal the locations and characteristics
of potentially private infrastructure and activities.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{tesla_gigafactory_private_mcc_map.pdf}
    \caption{Testing and private MCC identities reveal sensitive commercial infrastructure and activities.}
    \label{fig:commercial-sensitive-mcc-maps}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:commercial-sensitive-mcc-maps} shows that the same exposure
extends to commercial and critical infrastructure. Private MCC identities
delineate networks at the Cadia mine (11 LTE identities; 5,334 observations),
Tesla Gigafactory Berlin (20 NR identities; 8,865 observations), the Applus+
IDIADA automotive proving ground (11 GSM/UMTS identities; 4,622 observations),
and Point Beach Nuclear Plant (four NR identities; 760 observations). These
records reveal not only the presence of internal cellular systems, but also
their technology generation, physical footprint, and persistence—information
that may disclose facility capabilities, testing activity, or operational
changes. Thus, even when an MCC is correctly used for a private network, making
its observations globally discoverable has privacy implications for the
organization operating it.

Figure~\ref{fig:military-defense-mcc-maps} demonstrates that atypical MCCs can
act as signatures of otherwise non-public infrastructure. We observe an
unassigned 009/09 NR identity at the DGA Landes missile test range (578
observations), testing and unassigned identities at Idaho National Laboratory
(five identities; 1,033 observations), six testing 001/01 LTE identities at
Porton Down (688 observations), and five testing 001/01 LTE and NR identities at
Stockbridge (1,215 observations). Their repeated observation and spatial
concentration within research and defense facilities reveal the location, radio
technology, and approximate extent of networks that may have been intended only
for local or experimental use.

\subsection{Out-of-Country \acp{MCC}}

Valid nationally assigned MCC can be still be a signature of adversarial
activity when it appears outside the country that administers it. Although some
mismatches arise from legacy allocations or identifier aliasing, spatially
concentrated mismatches can be a side effect of private or sensitive sites and
activities. We analyzed a variety of examples where out-of-country MCC placement
could be used to discover unusual operator activity.

%\begin{figure*}[t]
%    \centering
%    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{out_of_country_mcc_matrix.pdf}
%    \caption{Out-of-country cellular identities more than 25\,km beyond their
%    assigned \ac{MCC} territory concentrate in a small number of located-country
%    and MCC-country pairs.}
%    \label{fig:out-of-country-mcc-matrix}
%\end{figure*}

After excluding identities located within their assigned MCC territory or within
25\,km of its boundary, we retain 253,484 distinct cell identities across 1,261
located-country–MCC-country pairs. Figure~\ref{fig:out-of-country-mcc-matrix}
shows that this distribution is highly concentrated: Russian MCCs in Ukraine
account for 77,805 identities, followed by U.S. MCCs in Puerto Rico (44,773),
Jamaican MCCs in Haiti (20,587), and Monaco MCCs in Kosovo (17,314). These
mismatches have heterogeneous explanations, including conflict-related network
deployment, territorial and legacy allocations, identifier aliasing, database
errors, and deliberate network emulation. The matrix therefore serves as a
screening mechanism rather than evidence of adversarial activity: large
concentrations reveal systematic phenomena, while the long tail identifies
smaller cases requiring spatial and temporal investigation.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{geopolitical_defense_mcc_maps.pdf}
    \caption{Foreign MCC identities at four military and training sites.}
    \label{fig:geopolitical-defense-mcc-maps}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:geopolitical-defense-mcc-maps} illustrates why even
low-frequency mismatches can be security-relevant when situated in sensitive
facilities. We observe one Azerbaijani GSM identity at an IRGC command facility
in Tehran (81 observations), one Ukrainian GSM identity at a military unit near
Chkalovsky in Moscow Oblast (88 observations), ten LTE identities carrying
Czech, Armenian, Bahraini, and Singaporean MCCs at the Muscatatuck Urban
Training Center (1,309 observations), and 27 GSM and LTE identities carrying
Tuvalu’s MCC at Fort Bragg (3,888 observations). The multi-country concentration
at a training facility is consistent with network emulation, while the coherent
Tuvalu-coded deployment at Fort Bragg suggests a locally configured system
rather than ordinary roaming. The two isolated identities may instead reflect
transient testing, misconfiguration, geolocation error, or deliberate
manipulation; nevertheless, their detectability demonstrates how foreign MCCs
can expose signals activity at locations of military or geopolitical interest.

\subsection{GNSS Spoofing}

GNSS spoofing is the deliberate transmission of counterfeit satellite-navigation
signals that cause a receiver to calculate an incorrect position, velocity, or
time. A spoofer generally generates signals that resemble authentic GNSS
broadcasts but are stronger, delayed, or otherwise modified so that the receiver
locks onto the counterfeit constellation. By gradually changing the counterfeit
signals, an attacker can move the reported position smoothly; by switching
between signal configurations, the attacker can create abrupt jumps or recurring
false locations. Spoofing differs from jamming: jamming prevents a receiver from
obtaining a usable GNSS fix, whereas spoofing supplies a plausible but incorrect
one.

We show this activity can be indirectly discovered through Apple’s cellular
positioning system. Devices collecting cellular identifiers associate nearby
cells with their own estimated locations and upload those observations to
Apple’s database. If the collector’s GNSS position is spoofed, genuine cellular
identifiers can be recorded at a false location, causing the database position
of a fixed cell or group of cells to migrate. This mechanism does not provide
absolute proof of an RF spoofing transmission, since similar patterns can arise
from geolocation errors, or transported equipment. We therefore treat
coordinated, geographically implausible, and temporally reversible movements as
evidence to be evaluated alongside these alternatives.

\subsection{Criminal Activity}

Scam compounds are organized criminal facilities in which workers conduct online
fraud at scale. Recruitment often involves deceptive employment offers followed
by coercion, trafficking, or debt bondage. Inside the compounds, captives
execute a variety of investment and impersonation scams. Reports describe
restricted movement, confiscated identity documents, surveillance, threats, and
violence used to enforce compliance and prevent escape. In recent years these
organizations have expanded across Southeast Asia, principally in Cambodia,
Myanmar, and Laos~\cite{unodc-casinos-cyber-fraud-2023}. These enterprises are
interesting targets for Internet-measurement because documented compounds have
increasingly deployed bespoke connectivity. While they may prefer linking to
fiber networks serving casinos, hotels, resorts, etc. local authorities have
responded by cutting their internet, so some scam operations have turned to
alternative strategies like smuggled Starlink terminals, clandestine cables, and
mobile base stations~\cite{unodc-casinos-cyber-fraud-2023}.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{criminal_compound_quad.png}
    \caption{Full-color imagery shows the four primary Cambodia 404/01 LTE TAC-0 site-confined clusters and their observed identifier locations.}
    \label{fig:criminal-compound-quad}
\end{figure}

We began our search for anomolous infrastructure in Cambodia, for which our
corpus contains 139.1 million observations of 315,264 unique cell-identity
records. About 1.8 million observations (1.29\%) utilized out-of-country MCCs
comprising 5,195 unique records, which was a managable quantity to triage
manually. One anomaly that immediately stood out was that around 5\% of them
broadcast an Indian MCC and (more oddly) a Tracking-Area-Code (TAC) value of 0.
All the identities were spatially clustered at several locations around the
country, which we reverse geocoded and cross referenced with human rights
reports that documented suspected compounds. Of the 7 clusters we identified, 5
had been classified by Amnesty International as "confirmed" scam compounds
(named KA01, KA03, TD01H, BM03, and BA01) and the remaining 2 were specifically
identified as "suspicious locations" (named CT12 and BA23) on account of
physical and organizational security features resembling known
compounds~\cite{amnesty-cambodia-scams-2025,amnesty-cambodia-scams-2026}. Our
collection observed the unusual 404/01–TAC-0 configuration nowhere else in
Cambodia outside the five confirmed scam compounds and two suspected ones, which
we belive supports the view the latter should be subject to further
investigation.

\begin{figure}[p]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{criminal_compound_additional.png}
    \caption{Full-color imagery shows the three additional Cambodia 404/01 LTE TAC-0 clusters omitted from the primary-compound quad.}
    \label{fig:criminal-compound-additional}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:criminal-compound-quad} shows four compounds denoted KA03, KA01,
BA01, and TD01H by Amnesty International with the corresponding cellular
observations. KA03 and KA01 are secured facilities on Bokor Mountain where
survivors reported deceptive recruitment, forced work, and physical abuse. At
BA01 in Bavet, survivors described confinement behind fences and abuse by
guards. TD01H, in the Thma Da special economic zone, was associated with
reported rescues and later evidence of fencing and guarded checkpoints.
Figure~\ref{fig:criminal-compound-additional} shows BM03, CT12, and BA23, which
exhibit the same unusual cellular signature. BM03, an alleged casino and
call-center complex, was associated with rescues and security features including
fences, cameras, and internal
checkpoints~\cite{amnesty-cambodia-scams-2025,amnesty-cambodia-scams-2026}.

\subsection{Contested Territories}

Cellular infrastructure can reveal adversarial activity beyond attacks on the
network itself, because any event that impacts network operation and
availability may leave observable infrastructure-level signatures. One example
of this phenomena is terratorial sovereignty: political control over a region
includes its communications infrastructure and carries the responsibility for
its administration. Thus, the geographic distribution of cellular activity can
implicate political control over those regions, which is a particularly useful
basis for investigating regions subject to competing claims of sovereignty.

We examine the geographic and temporal distribution of cellular activity in
contested regions of Eastern Europe and The Levant. In Ukraine, we observe that
Russian administered cellular infrastructure follows the front lines
geographically.
